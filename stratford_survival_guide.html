01 · Welcome to SPB

Middle school is a new adventure. You’ve got this.

Welcome new Lions to Stratford Preparatory Blackford! The work gets a little tougher, but there are new friends to meet, hobbies to discover, and opportunities to try. The goal is to reach your Summa Spes — your highest hope.

Use this guide to get familiar with campus life, know who to ask for help, explore clubs, prepare for classes, and set goals. SPB is both a middle school and a high school, so you may see older students around campus who are out of uniform.

02 · Your compass

R.E.A.C.H. values

Small choices every day can help you become a great student and a good citizen.

01—05
R.

Respect

Treat everyone around you with respect. Help others and greet people kindly — even a simple “good morning” matters.

E.

Excellence

Try your best at everything you do. Give assignments a final check and put in the effort to do quality work.

A.

Always Improving

Look for ways to grow when something is difficult. If a grade disappoints you, learn what you can do differently next time.

C.

Collaborative Culture

Work with others, listen to different perspectives, and make space for everyone’s ideas during group projects.

H.

High Expectations

Set ambitious goals for yourself. For a test, choose a goal that challenges you and see how close you can get.

04 · Everyday basics

Know the rules. Make life easier.

🏫 School rules from the slideshow

  • The slideshow says students should have no more than two tests per day. If you have more, report it to administration; verify the current policy with staff.
  • The Learning Commons (library) is accessible during lunch and break.
  • Chromebooks may be used in class only when the teacher permits it; they can also be used at home.
Policies can change. Ask a teacher or administrator if you are unsure what the current rule is.

💻 Chromebook care

  • Be respectful online and do not visit inappropriate or unreasonable sites.
  • Do not intentionally throw or drop your Chromebook. Take care of it.
  • Keep food and liquids away from the device.
  • The slideshow says Blocksi is enabled and some websites may be blocked.
  • Avoid staying on your Chromebook past midnight; sleep helps you learn.
  • The slideshow says students keep their Chromebook through 8th grade, so protect it and avoid losing it.

06 · Move & participate

P.E. without the panic.

Use the steps from the student slideshow to prepare for P.E. class.

👟 P.E. routine

  • Wait outside the gym until a teacher lets the class in.
  • Go to the appropriate locker room, change into your P.E. uniform, and drop off your bag. The slideshow says you have five minutes.
  • When you are ready, go to the gymnasium and wait where your coach directs you.
  • Do not buy anything from the vending machines during this routine; the slideshow says you can do so after class.

👕 P.E. uniform notes

  • You may wear your P.E. uniform to school, but change out of it after your P.E. period.
  • The slideshow also lists a plain grey shirt and navy shorts or pants as an option.
  • Check with your teacher for current uniform and dress-code requirements.

07 · Class survival

Study smarter, not just longer.

These subject strategies come from the original slideshow, with a few practical reminders to help you use them.

➗

Math

Test yourself instead of only rereading. The slideshow suggests using tools such as Gemini Notebook to make practice quizzes; follow your teacher’s rules about AI tools.

🧪

Science

Annotate your textbook and draw diagrams to help with memorization. Rereading can also help when you do it actively.

🏛️

History

Take clear notes and draw timelines or diagrams to connect events and see how they fit together.

📚

ELA

Use short quizzes and flashcards to build memorization. Reading also helps grow vocabulary.

💻

Computer Science

Ask your teacher for help with coding and learning how to use applications when you get stuck.

🌎

Language Studies

Language apps such as Duolingo can help with vocabulary. Study words and look for common English roots and meanings, too.

⏳ Time management

  • Plan when you will work and when each assignment is due.
  • Prioritize: decide what is due first and what matters most.
  • Avoid distractions such as YouTube, scrolling, or multitasking while doing homework.
  • Do not overwork yourself. Work hard in moderation so you do not burn out.
  • Finish homework and test studying first, then make time to relax.
  • Pack your bag the night before and keep track of deadlines.

📈 Grades: what the slideshow says

These are the ranges written in the original student slideshow, not a verified current grading policy.

  • A+ : 98–100
  • A : 92–97
  • A− : 90–91
  • B+ : 86–89
  • The slideshow says below 85 is failing in Math, and 82% is the minimum for other classes.
Ask your teacher for the official grading scale for each class. A grade is feedback, not a measure of your worth.
08 · Speak up

Asking for help is a skill.

Your teachers want to know when you are confused. Keep questions clear, polite, and specific.

💬 Asking in person

  • Choose a suitable time and place.
  • Organize your thoughts so you can explain the problem clearly.
  • Listen to the answer and avoid interrupting.
  • Ask a follow-up if something still does not make sense.

✉️ Email outline

  • Use a clear, specific subject line.
  • Start with a professional greeting, such as “Dear Mr. Chu,” and a polite opening.
  • State your name and which class you are in; teachers teach multiple classes.
  • Explain the reason for your email clearly and briefly.
  • Close politely, then check spelling and grammar.

Example email you can adapt

Use this as a template and replace the bracketed details.

Subject: Question about [assignment or topic]

Dear [Teacher’s Name],

I hope you are doing well. My name is [Your Name] from your [class name] class. I am writing because I have a question about [specific assignment or concept]. I have tried [what you have already tried], but I am still confused about [specific part]. Could you please explain what I should do next?

Thank you for your time and help.

Best,
[Your Name]

10 · Advisory

A place to connect and reset.

What happens in advisory?

  • You meet with your two main teachers.
  • You talk about how to put R.E.A.C.H. values into practice and may make projects about them.
  • It is a chance to bond with classmates, talk, and include people.
  • Your class representative may share upcoming events and class updates.

🧭 Be a good classmate

Say hello, include someone in a conversation, listen when others share, and be open to different viewpoints. You do not need to know everyone on day one — build connections little by little.

13 · Locker confidence

Get organized. Move calmly.

Practice your combination before the school day gets busy.

🔐 Combination-lock steps from the slideshow

  1. Spin the dial around a couple of times to reset it.
  2. Turn left to your first number.
  3. Turn right, go around one full round, then stop at your second number.
  4. Turn left again to your third number.
Follow the instructions on your specific lock and ask a staff member to demonstrate if it behaves differently.

🎒 Locker tips

  • Do not leave food in your locker; it can rot and attract ants or spiders.
  • Use your locker during passing periods, breaks, and lunch as allowed.
  • Keep materials organized so you can find them quickly.
  • Go slowly while you are learning the combination. Practice before you are in a rush.
14 · Real talk

Myths vs. reality.

“Middle-school grades do not matter at all.”
The slideshow’s reality check is that grades do matter for learning and future preparation, but they do not define you as a person. Use grades as feedback and ask for help when you need it.
“If you are late once, you will immediately get detention.”
The slideshow says a single tardy does not automatically mean detention. Still, sign in and follow the school’s tardy procedure, and work on getting to class on time.
“Everyone will act weird because they are growing up.”
People change and find new interests at different times. Be respectful, do not assume everyone is the same, and give people room to be themselves.
